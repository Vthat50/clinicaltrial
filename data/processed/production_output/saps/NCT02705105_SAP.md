## Sample Size

**Sample Size Justification:**

For this Phase 1/2 open-label, multicenter study of mogamulizumab, the sample size is based on the exploratory nature of early-phase development rather than formal statistical hypothesis testing.

**Phase 1 Dose Escalation Component:**
The Phase 1 portion follows a standard 3+3 dose escalation design to determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) and recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D). Approximately 18-24 patients are expected to be enrolled across 3-4 dose levels, with 3-6 patients per cohort depending on dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) occurrence.

**Phase 2 Expansion Component:**
The null hypothesis (H0) states that the overall response rate (ORR) is ≤10% (historical control rate). The alternative hypothesis (H1) states that the ORR is ≥30% (clinically meaningful response rate). Using Simon's two-stage optimal design with alpha = 0.05 (one-sided) and power = 80% (1-beta = 0.80), the expected effect size corresponds to an ORR improvement from 10% to 30% (difference of 20 percentage points). 

In Stage 1, 10 patients will be enrolled. If ≤1 response is observed, the study will be terminated for futility. If ≥2 responses are observed, an additional 19 patients will be enrolled in Stage 2 (total n=29). The regimen will be considered promising if ≥6 responses are observed among 29 evaluable patients.

Assuming a 10% dropout rate for inevaluable patients, a total of 32 patients will be enrolled in the Phase 2 portion. Combined with the Phase 1 component, the total enrollment target is approximately 56 patients.

Sample size calculations were performed using EAST 6.5 software.

## ICH E9(R1) Estimand Framework

### Primary Estimand (Phase 2 Component)
- **Treatment:** Mogamulizumab administered intravenously at the recommended Phase 2 dose determined from Phase 1
- **Population:** Patients with relapsed/refractory cutaneous T-cell lymphoma eligible for treatment
- **Variable:** Overall response rate (ORR) defined as proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) per investigator assessment using modified Severity Weighted Assessment Tool (mSWAT)
- **Intercurrent events:** Treatment discontinuation due to disease progression, adverse events, or withdrawal will be handled as treatment failure in the primary analysis
- **Summary measure:** Difference in response rates between observed rate and historical control rate

### Secondary Estimand (Safety Run-in Phase 1)
- **Treatment:** Mogamulizumab dose escalation following 3+3 design
- **Population:** Patients with advanced hematologic malignancies
- **Variable:** Dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) rate in first treatment cycle
- **Intercurrent events:** Early discontinuation for reasons other than DLT will be replaced to ensure adequate safety evaluation
- **Summary measure:** Proportion of patients experiencing DLT at each dose level

## Primary Statistical Analysis

### Phase 1 Dose Escalation Analysis
The primary endpoint for Phase 1 is safety and determination of recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D). DLT rates will be analyzed using a traditional 3+3 dose escalation design. At each dose level, the proportion of patients experiencing DLT in Cycle 1 will be calculated with exact 95% confidence intervals using the Clopper-Pearson method. The RP2D will be defined as the highest dose level at which ≤1 of 6 patients experience DLT, provided the next higher dose level has ≥2 of 3-6 patients with DLT. All safety analyses will be descriptive without formal hypothesis testing.

### Phase 2 Efficacy Analysis
The primary endpoint for Phase 2 is overall response rate (ORR). The primary analysis will use a one-sample exact binomial test comparing the observed ORR to a pre-specified historical control rate of 15%. The null hypothesis H₀: p ≤ 0.15 will be tested against the alternative hypothesis H₁: p > 0.30 using a one-sided test at alpha = 0.10. The exact 95% confidence interval for ORR will be calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method. If the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeds 15%, the treatment will be considered promising for further development.

A logistic regression model will be fitted as a secondary analysis to explore potential predictive factors:
```
logit(P(response)) = β₀ + β₁(baseline ECOG PS) + β₂(prior therapies) + β₃(disease subtype)
```

## Secondary Analyses

### Time-to-Event Endpoints
Duration of response, progression-free survival, and overall survival will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methodology. Median time-to-event with 95% confidence intervals will be estimated using the Brookmeyer-Crowley method. For progression-free survival, a one-sample log-rank test will compare observed survival to historical controls, with hazard ratios estimated using Cox proportional hazards regression.

### Safety Analyses
All safety analyses will be descriptive and based on the safety population (all patients receiving ≥1 dose of study drug). Adverse events will be coded using MedDRA version 24.0 and graded per NCI CTCAE version 4.03. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) will be summarized by system organ class, preferred term, and maximum grade. Laboratory parameters will be analyzed using shift tables and plots over time.

## Handling of Missing Data

**Primary Approach:** For the primary efficacy endpoint (ORR), patients without adequate post-baseline tumor assessment will be considered non-responders (conservative approach under missing not at random assumption).

**Sensitivity Analyses:** 
1. Complete case analysis excluding patients with missing response data
2. Multiple imputation under missing at random (MAR) assumption using logistic regression including baseline covariates
3. Tipping point analysis evaluating robustness to assumptions about missing data mechanism

For time-to-event endpoints, patients will be censored at last known alive date or last tumor assessment date. Sensitivity analyses will include alternative censoring rules and pattern mixture models to assess impact of informative censoring.

## Multiplicity Considerations

Given the exploratory nature of this Phase 1/2 study, no formal multiplicity adjustments will be applied to secondary endpoints. All secondary analyses will be considered exploratory and interpreted with appropriate caution. The primary Phase 2 endpoint will be tested at the pre-specified one-sided alpha = 0.10 level.

For multiple safety endpoints in Phase 1, the Bayesian continual reassessment method will be considered for future dose escalation decisions, but the primary analysis will follow the traditional 3+3 design without multiplicity adjustment.

## Interim Analyses

An interim analysis for futility will be conducted after 50% of planned Phase 2 patients complete their first response assessment. Using a beta-binomial Bayesian approach with non-informative prior (Beta(0.5, 0.5)), the study may be stopped for futility if the posterior probability of achieving the target response rate falls below 10%.

## Statistical Software

All analyses will be performed using **SAS version 9.4** or higher. Kaplan-Meier plots and forest plots will be generated using SAS/GRAPH procedures. R version 4.2 or higher may be used for specialized Bayesian analyses and advanced visualization.

## Analysis Populations

- **Safety Population:** All patients who received ≥1 dose of mogamulizumab
- **Efficacy Population (Phase 2):** All patients in Phase 2 who received ≥1 dose and had baseline measurable disease
- **Per-Protocol Population:** All efficacy population patients without major protocol deviations affecting efficacy assessment

## Analysis Populations

**Analysis Populations**

**Intent-to-Treat (ITT) Population:** All enrolled patients will be included in the ITT population and analyzed according to assigned treatment, regardless of actual treatment received or study completion status. This serves as the primary population for all efficacy analyses in this Phase 1/2 study.

**Modified Intent-to-Treat (mITT) Population:** All enrolled patients who received at least one dose of mogamulizumab and had baseline disease assessment will be included in the mITT population. Patients will be analyzed according to assigned treatment. This population will be used for supportive efficacy analyses and sensitivity analyses.

**Per-Protocol (PP) Population:** All patients in the mITT population without major protocol deviations that could affect efficacy evaluation. Major protocol deviations leading to exclusion from the PP population include:
- Use of prohibited concomitant medications that could affect efficacy outcomes
- Treatment compliance <80% of planned dose intensity (for Phase 2 portion)
- Missing critical efficacy assessments that preclude response evaluation
- Incorrect study drug administration outside protocol-specified parameters
- Protocol violations related to inclusion/exclusion criteria discovered post-enrollment

**Safety Population:** All patients who received at least one dose (complete or partial) of mogamulizumab will be included in the safety population and analyzed according to actual treatment received. This is the primary population for all safety and tolerability analyses, including adverse events, laboratory parameters, and dose-limiting toxicities.

**Primary Analysis Population:** The ITT population serves as the primary analysis population for efficacy endpoints. The Safety population serves as the primary analysis population for all safety endpoints.

**Handling of Protocol Deviations and Discontinuations:** All enrolled patients will be included in analyses according to the intent-to-treat principle. Patients who discontinue treatment or withdraw from the study will be included in analyses up to the point of discontinuation. Missing data will be handled according to predefined missing data strategies outlined in the statistical analysis plan. Protocol deviations will be classified as major or minor, with major deviations potentially leading to exclusion from the Per-Protocol population only.

## Primary Efficacy Analysis

**Note:** No primary efficacy endpoint was specified in the protocol for this Phase 1/2 dose-escalation and dose-expansion study of mogamulizumab. The primary objectives focus on safety, tolerability, and determination of the recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D).

## Secondary Efficacy Analyses

Given the Phase 1/2 nature of this study, secondary efficacy endpoints will be analyzed as exploratory objectives to provide preliminary evidence of clinical activity:

### Overall Response Rate (ORR)

**Null Hypothesis (H0):** The true overall response rate equals the historical control rate (π₀ = 0.10).

**Alternative Hypothesis (H1):** The true overall response rate differs from the historical control rate (π ≠ 0.10).

The point estimate of ORR will be calculated as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to applicable disease-specific response criteria. The 95% confidence interval will be constructed using the Clopper-Pearson exact binomial method. Response rates will be summarized by dose level and indication, with 95% confidence intervals provided.

### Progression-Free Survival (PFS)

**Null Hypothesis (H0):** The hazard ratio for progression-free survival equals 1.0 compared to historical controls.

**Alternative Hypothesis (H1):** The hazard ratio differs from 1.0.

PFS will be analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method. Median PFS and corresponding 95% confidence intervals will be estimated using the Brookmeyer-Crowley method. The analysis will be descriptive given the single-arm design and small sample sizes typical of Phase 1/2 studies.

### Duration of Response (DOR)

For patients achieving objective response, duration of response will be calculated from the date of first documented response until disease progression or death. DOR will be summarized using descriptive statistics and Kaplan-Meier methodology.

## Subgroup Analyses

Pre-specified exploratory subgroup analyses will be performed for the following factors:
- Age groups (<65 vs ≥65 years)

- ECOG performance status (0-1 vs 2+)
- Prior lines of therapy (1-2 vs 3+ prior regimens)
- Disease histology/indication (if multiple indications enrolled)

Results will be presented in forest plots showing point estimates and 95% confidence intervals for each subgroup. Interaction tests will be performed using Fisher's exact test for categorical endpoints and log-rank tests for time-to-event endpoints, though these analyses will be considered exploratory given the limited sample size.

Anti-drug antibody (ADA) formation will be assessed if applicable. Safety run-in data will be presented separately from expansion cohorts where relevant to the study design.

## Missing Data

# Missing Data Handling

## Primary Approach
The primary analysis will assume missing data follows a Missing at Random (MAR) mechanism. Given the Phase 1/2 design of this mogamulizumab study, a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM) will be employed as the primary analytical framework. This approach naturally accommodates missing data under the MAR assumption by utilizing all available observations without requiring imputation.

The MMRM will include the following covariates:
- Treatment group (if applicable in Phase 2 portion)
- Study visit (time point)
- Treatment-by-visit interaction
- Baseline efficacy measurement
- Relevant stratification factors (disease stage, prior therapy lines, ECOG performance status)

## Missing Data Assumptions
- **Primary assumption**: Missing at Random (MAR) - missingness depends on observed data but not on unobserved values
- **Alternative scenarios**: Missing Not at Random (MNAR) will be explored through sensitivity analyses
- Missing Completely at Random (MCAR) will be tested using Little's MCAR test where appropriate

## Sensitivity Analyses

### 1. Multiple Imputation under MAR
Missing efficacy values will be imputed using multiple imputation with m=100 imputations to ensure adequate precision. The imputation model will include:
- All observed efficacy measurements
- Treatment assignment and baseline characteristics
- Auxiliary variables predictive of missingness and outcomes

### 2. Tipping Point Analysis
Systematic sensitivity analysis will assess the robustness of efficacy conclusions by:
- Progressively worsening imputed values for patients with missing data
- Determining the delta-adjustment threshold that would change study conclusions
- Applied separately by treatment group and time point

### 3. Pattern Mixture Models
Missing data patterns will be classified and analyzed separately to explore MNAR scenarios:
- Early discontinuation pattern (≤4 weeks)
- Mid-study discontinuation pattern (>4 weeks to <12 weeks)
- Late discontinuation pattern (≥12 weeks)
- Completion pattern

### 4. Jump to Reference Imputation
For patients discontinuing due to:
- Progressive disease: Impute outcomes assuming continued disease progression
- Adverse events: Impute using outcomes from historical control data
- Other reasons: Impute assuming no further treatment benefit

### 5. Worst-case/Best-case Scenarios
- **Worst-case**: Missing responses assumed as non-responders; missing continuous outcomes imputed at worst observed value
- **Best-case**: Missing responses assumed as responders; missing continuous outcomes imputed at best observed value

## Multiplicity

# Multiplicity Adjustments

## Overall Strategy

Given that this is an open-label, multicenter Phase 1/2 study of mogamulizumab, the multiplicity adjustment strategy will be tailored to the exploratory nature of early-phase clinical development while maintaining appropriate statistical rigor for key efficacy and safety evaluations.

**Phase 1 Component:** No formal multiplicity adjustments will be applied to safety endpoints during the dose-escalation phase, as the primary focus is on dose-finding and safety characterization. All safety analyses will be descriptive and exploratory in nature.

**Phase 2 Component:** For efficacy analyses in the expansion phase, a controlled Type I error approach will be implemented as described below.

## Type I Error Control Strategy

The overall familywise Type I error rate will be controlled at the two-sided 0.05 level using a sequential gatekeeping procedure for the Phase 2 efficacy analyses. This approach acknowledges the exploratory nature of the study while providing appropriate statistical control for the most clinically relevant endpoints.

## Alpha Allocation and Testing Hierarchy

### Primary Efficacy Testing Sequence:
1. **Overall Response Rate (ORR)** - Tested first at α = 0.05 (two-sided)
2. **Duration of Response (DOR)** - Tested at α = 0.05 only if ORR achieves statistical significance
3. **Progression-Free Survival (PFS)** - Tested at α = 0.05 only if DOR achieves statistical significance

### Secondary Efficacy Endpoints:
Secondary efficacy endpoints will be tested using the Hochberg step-up procedure at α = 0.05, contingent upon successful completion of the primary testing sequence. The secondary endpoints will be ranked by clinical importance as follows:

1. Overall Survival (OS)
2. Complete Response Rate (CRR)
3. Disease Control Rate (DCR)
4. Time to Response (TTR)

## Adjustment Methods

- **Primary Testing Sequence:** Sequential gatekeeping procedure (fixed sequence method)
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Hochberg step-up procedure
- **Safety Endpoints:** No formal adjustments; all analyses descriptive and exploratory
- **Subgroup Analyses:** No formal adjustments; all analyses exploratory

## Special Considerations

### Dose Cohorts in Phase 1:
No multiplicity adjustments will be applied across dose cohorts during the dose-escalation phase. Each dose cohort will be evaluated independently for safety, with dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) rates assessed descriptively.

### Biomarker Analyses:
All biomarker analyses will be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating. No formal multiplicity adjustments will be applied to biomarker-defined subgroup analyses.

### Interim Safety Reviews:
Safety data will be continuously monitored throughout the study. No alpha adjustment is required for ongoing safety analyses, as these are conducted for patient safety rather than formal hypothesis testing.

## Implementation Notes

- All confidence intervals for secondary endpoints will be presented as nominal (unadjusted) values with appropriate caveats regarding multiplicity
- P-values for secondary endpoints will be clearly labeled as nominal or adjusted based on the testing hierarchy results
- Any post-hoc analyses will be clearly identified as exploratory with no multiplicity adjustments applied

This multiplicity strategy balances the need for statistical rigor in efficacy assessment while maintaining the flexibility appropriate for early-phase drug development and the exploratory objectives inherent in Phase 1/2 study designs.

## Sensitivity Analyses

# Sensitivity Analyses

The following sensitivity analyses will be performed to assess the robustness of the primary and key secondary analyses:

## 1. Alternative Analysis Populations

**1.1 Per-Protocol Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To evaluate treatment effects in patients who adhered to the protocol without major deviations
- **Method**: Primary efficacy endpoints will be re-analyzed excluding patients with major protocol deviations (>20% dose reduction, treatment discontinuation due to non-medical reasons, or prohibited concomitant medications)
- **Assessment**: Results will be compared with the intention-to-treat analysis to evaluate consistency

**1.2 Evaluable Population Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To assess efficacy in patients with adequate follow-up and evaluable disease assessments
- **Method**: Analysis restricted to patients with baseline assessments and at least one post-baseline efficacy evaluation
- **Assessment**: Robustness of efficacy conclusions across different analysis populations

## 2. Alternative Statistical Methods

**2.1 Non-parametric Approaches**
- **Rationale**: To avoid distributional assumptions in survival analyses
- **Method**: 
  - Log-rank tests for time-to-event endpoints instead of Cox regression
  - Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for continuous endpoints
- **Assessment**: Comparison of p-values and effect size estimates

**2.2 Alternative Confidence Interval Methods**
- **Rationale**: To assess robustness of interval estimates, particularly for small sample sizes
- **Method**: Bootstrap confidence intervals (2000 resamples) for key efficacy parameters
- **Assessment**: Comparison of bootstrap CIs with normal approximation CIs

## 3. Missing Data Sensitivity Analyses

**3.1 Complete Case Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To assess impact of missing data imputation assumptions
- **Method**: Analysis restricted to patients with complete data for each endpoint
- **Assessment**: Comparison with primary analysis using all available data

**3.2 Pattern Mixture Models**
- **Rationale**: To explore missing not at random (MNAR) assumptions
- **Method**: Separate models for different missing data patterns, assuming worse outcomes for patients with missing data
- **Assessment**: Evaluation under pessimistic missing data scenarios

**3.3 Sensitivity to Censoring Assumptions**
- **Rationale**: To assess impact of different censoring rules on survival endpoints
- **Method**: Alternative censoring at:
  - Last known alive date instead of last contact
  - Administrative censoring at different time points
- **Assessment**: Comparison of median survival estimates and hazard ratios

## 4. Dose-Response Sensitivity Analyses

**4.1 Actual Dose Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To account for dose modifications and cumulative exposure
- **Method**: Analysis based on actual received dose rather than planned dose
- **Assessment**: Correlation between cumulative dose and efficacy outcomes

**4.2 Dose Intensity Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To evaluate impact of dose delays and reductions
- **Method**: Stratified analysis by dose intensity categories (≥90%, 75-89%, <75% of planned dose)
- **Assessment**: Consistency of treatment effects across dose intensity strata

## 5. Subgroup Robustness Assessments

**5.1 Baseline Characteristic Adjustment**
- **Rationale**: To assess consistency across different patient characteristics
- **Method**: Multivariate models adjusting for:
  - Age (≥65 vs <65 years)
  - ECOG performance status (0-1 vs ≥2)
  - Prior lines of therapy (≤2 vs >2)
  - Disease stage at baseline
- **Assessment**: Forest plots showing treatment effects across subgroups

**5.2 Center Effect Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To evaluate consistency across participating sites
- **Method**: Mixed-effects models with center as random effect
- **Assessment**: Between-center heterogeneity assessment using I² statistic

## 6. Time-Dependent Sensitivity Analyses

**6.1 Landmark Analysis**
- **Rationale**: To address guarantee-time bias in survival analyses
- **Method**: Landmark analyses at 3, 6, and 12 months for overall survival
- **Assessment**: Consistency of treatment effects at different landmark times

**6.2 Time-Varying Coefficient Models**
- **Rationale**: To assess proportional hazards assumption
- **Method**: Cox models with time-dependent coefficients
- **Assessment**: Schoenfeld residual plots and tests for non-proportionality

## 7. Safety Sensitivity Analyses

**7.1 Treatment-Emergent vs All-Causality Events**
- **Rationale**: To distinguish treatment-related from disease-related events
- **Method**: Separate analyses for treatment-emergent AEs and all reported AEs
- **Assessment**: Comparison of safety profiles under different attribution criteria

**7.2 Exposure-Adjusted Incidence Rates**
- **Rationale**: To account for different exposure durations
- **Method**: AE rates per patient-year of exposure
- **Assessment**: Time-to-first-event analysis for key safety endpoints

## 8. Multiplicity Sensitivity

**8.1 Alternative Multiple Comparison Adjustments**
- **Rationale**: To assess robustness to different multiplicity control methods
- **Method**: 
  - Bonferroni correction
  - False discovery rate (FDR) control
  - Hierarchical testing procedures
- **Assessment**: Impact on statistical significance of key findings

## 9. Robustness Assessment Criteria

For each sensitivity analysis, the following criteria will be used to assess robustness:
- **Quantitative consistency**: Effect estimates within 20% of primary analysis
- **Qualitative consistency**: Same direction of effect and similar clinical interpretation
- **Statistical consistency**: Overlapping confidence intervals with primary analysis
- **Clinical relevance**: Maintained clinical significance of findings

Results of sensitivity analyses will be summarized in tabular format showing effect estimates, confidence intervals, and p-values compared to the primary analysis, with narrative interpretation of the robustness of key study conclusions.

## Interim Analyses

# Interim Analyses

**Timing:** Given the Phase 1/2 design of this open-label, multicenter study of mogamulizumab, interim analyses will be conducted at the completion of Phase 1 dose-escalation portion and when approximately 50% of evaluable patients have been enrolled in the Phase 2 portion. Additional safety reviews may be conducted as deemed necessary by the Data Monitoring Committee.

**Alpha-Spending:** For the Phase 2 portion, a Lan-DeMets alpha-spending function with O'Brien-Fleming-type boundaries will be used to control the overall Type I error rate at 0.05 (two-sided). The Phase 1 portion will not have formal alpha-spending controls as it is primarily focused on safety and dose-finding.

**Stopping Boundaries:**
- **Efficacy:** The Phase 2 portion may be stopped early for efficacy if the observed treatment effect crosses the pre-specified efficacy boundary based on the primary endpoint.
- **Safety:** The study may be stopped or modified if unacceptable safety signals are observed, particularly during the Phase 1 dose-escalation portion.
- **Futility:** Non-binding futility boundaries will be calculated for the Phase 2 portion using conditional power methodology to assess the likelihood of achieving the primary endpoint.

**Data Monitoring Committee:** An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will review unblinded safety and efficacy data at each interim analysis. The DSMB will make recommendations regarding dose escalation (Phase 1), study continuation, modification, or early termination based on the benefit-risk assessment.

**Statistical Software:** Boundary calculations will be performed using EAST 6.5, SAS PROC SEQDESIGN, or equivalent validated statistical software. All calculations will be documented and verified prior to database lock.

## Subgroup Analyses

**Subgroup Analyses:**

**Pre-specified Subgroups:** The following subgroups are pre-specified for analysis:
- Age: <65 years vs ≥65 years
- Sex: Male vs Female
- ECOG Performance Status: 0 vs 1 vs ≥2
- Geographic Region: North America vs Europe vs Asia-Pacific
- Disease Stage: Early stage vs Advanced stage

**Presentation:** Results will be displayed in forest plots showing the effect estimate (hazard ratio for time-to-event endpoints, odds ratio for binary endpoints) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals for each subgroup level. Treatment-by-subgroup interaction p-values will be included for each subgroup analysis.

**Note:** All subgroup analyses are exploratory in nature and are not powered for formal hypothesis testing. No adjustments for multiplicity will be applied to subgroup analyses. Results should be interpreted with appropriate caution given the potential for chance findings due to multiple comparisons and reduced sample sizes within subgroup categories.
