# Protocol Amendment Synopsis

Protocol Title: A Multicenter, Double-Masked, Randomized, Dose-Ranging Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Conbercept Intravitreal Injection in Subjects with Neovascular Age-related Macular Degeneration

Protocol Number: KHB-1801, Version 3.0, Amendment #1

Study Medication: Conbercept for intravitreal (IVT) injection: 0.5 mg (10 mg/mL) and 1.0 mg (20 mg/mL)

Study Phase: 3

The purpose of this clinical study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of 0.5 mg and 1.0 mg conbercept IVT injection compared with the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) antagonist active control, aflibercept IVT injection (2.0 mg, Eylea®), in subjects with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (AMD).

# Primary Objective and Endpoint:

The primary objective is to determine if 0.5 mg or 1.0 mg conbercept is non-inferior to aflibercept 2.0 mg as measured by the change from baseline in best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) Early Treatment of Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letter score at the Week 36 visit.

The primary endpoint is the mean change from baseline in ETDRS BCVA letter score at Week 36 in the study eye.

# Key Secondary Objectives and Endpoints:

The secondary objectives of this study are:

1. To evaluate the difference in efficacy between conbercept doses and aflibercept, with respect to the following:

- Proportion of subjects maintaining vision (i.e., losing &#x3C;15 ETDRS BCVA letters) from baseline to Week 36
- Proportion of subjects gaining ≥15 ETDRS BCVA letters from baseline to Week 36;
- Mean change from baseline in central retinal thickness (μm) measured by SD-OCT at Week 36;

The study will randomize approximately 1140 subjects in a ratio of 1:1:1 to receive IVT injections of 0.5 mg conbercept, 1.0 mg conbercept, or 2.0 mg aflibercept. Subjects randomized to the conbercept treatment arms will receive an IVT injection of either 0.5 mg or 1.0 mg conbercept at 4-week intervals on Day 1, Week 4, and Week 8 (loading phase).

# Dosage/Treatment Frequency:

Subjects in the conbercept treatment 0.5 mg arm will begin dosing every 8 weeks (q8w) after the first three injections through Week 48. Beginning at the Week 52 visit, subjects will receive each visit evaluated criteria-based capped PRN dosing through the end of the treatment period at Week 92.

Subjects in the conbercept treatment 1.0 mg arm will begin dosing every 12 weeks (q12w) after the first three injections through Week 48. Beginning at the Week 52 visit, subjects will receive each visit evaluated criteria-based capped PRN dosing through the end of the treatment period at Week 92.

Aflibercept will be dosed as an IVT injection on Day 1, Week 4 and Week 8 and every 8 weeks (q8w) thereafter through Week 48. Beginning at Week 52 visit, subjects will receive each visit evaluated criteria-based capped PRN dosing through the end of the treatment period at Week 92.

All subjects will receive a sham procedure at visit intervals between each scheduled IVT injection and at visits where PRN criteria for re-treatment are not met, to mask any differences in dosing frequency.

# Sample Size

The study will randomize approximately 1140 subjects in a 1:1:1 ratio to the three study treatment arms.

The sample size is driven by the expectation that at least 300 subjects in each treatment group will complete 96 weeks on their assigned treatment. The primary efficacy endpoint is the mean change in ETDRS BCVA letter score from baseline at Week 36. If all three treatments are equally effective and if 90% percent of subjects in each treatment group complete the first 36 weeks of treatment, roughly 340 subjects in each group are expected to finish the study on their assigned study medication. Under these conditions, if this “completers” population data were to be used to test the primary outcome, the study would have 92% power to conclude non-inferiority to aflibercept of either the 0.5 mg conbercept dose or the 1.0 mg conbercept dose in the mean change from baseline in BCVA letters at Week 36, assuming a 2-sided Type I error rate of 0.025 (using Bonferroni correction for 2 comparisons with aflibercept group) for each test and a common standard deviation (SD) of 14.5 letters. If the two tests were independent, the probability that at least one conbercept dose will be shown non-inferior to aflibercept would be roughly 99%. Because the two tests are not independent (they share the same control group), the power to show at least one conbercept dose non-inferior to aflibercept will be between 92% and 99%. The actual statistical test will use a longitudinal model which is expected to decrease the width of the confidence interval.

# Endpoint Analysis

Primary and secondary efficacy endpoints in the study eye will be summarized using appropriate summary statistics, including appropriately sized confidence intervals (CIs) for each treatment and will be compared between each of the 2 test arms and the control arm. This Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) describes the methods to be used.

# 1. Introduction

This statistical analysis plan (SAP) defines the methods and analyses that Chengdu
Kanghong Biotechnology Company, henceforth referred to as Kanghong, will use to
analyze data from the KHB-1801 clinical research study. This SAP presents details of
the planned statistical methods addressing the study aims.

The amended SAP is based on Amendment 1 of the KHB-1801 study protocol dated
December 11, 2020. If the protocol is subsequently amended, this SAP may be
amended as well. The SAP describes the methods to be used in detail. If the SAP and
the protocol disagree, the details and methods of the SAP will prevail.

# 5. Sample Size Determination and Power Calculation

The primary hypothesis underlying the study is that each dose of conbercept will be non-inferior to aflibercept when the non-inferiority margin of the BCVA letter score measured by ETDRS is defined as -4 letters.

The sample size of the study is driven by the expectation that at least 300 subjects in each conbercept group will complete 96 weeks of evaluation on their assigned treatment. Experience from the VIEW 1 and VIEW 2 studies suggest that 16% of the subjects in each study group are likely to fail to finish 96 weeks of treatment on their assigned study drug [1]. If the study randomizes 380 participants in each treatment group and 16% participants in a treatment group fails to complete the planned 96 weeks on their assigned drug, that treatment group will have approximately 320 participants completing. Even if 20% of participants failed to complete 96 weeks of treatment in any or all of the treatment groups, the study group will have slightly over 300 completed subjects.

Therefore, the study will randomize approximately 1140 subjects in a 1:1:1 ratio to the three treatment groups. If all three treatments are equally effective and if 90% percent of subjects in each treatment group complete the first 36 weeks of treatment, roughly 340 subjects in each group are expected to finish the study on their assigned study medication. Under these conditions, if this “completers” population data were to be used to test the primary outcome, the study would have 92% power to conclude non-inferiority to aflibercept of either the 0.5 mg conbercept dose or the 1.0 mg conbercept dose in the mean change from baseline BCVA letters at Week 36, assuming a 2-sided Type I error rate of 0.025 (using Bonferroni correction for 2 comparisons with aflibercept group) for each test and a common standard deviation (SD) of 14.5 letters.

If the two tests were independent, the probability that at least one conbercept dose will be shown non-inferior to aflibercept would be roughly 99%. Because the two tests are not independent (they share the same control group), the power to show at least one conbercept dose non-inferior to aflibercept will be between 92% and 99%.

# 7. Statistical Analysis: General Considerations and Conventions

Descriptive and inferential statistics will be used to summarize results of protocol KHB-1801. Standard descriptive statistics, such as mean, standard deviation, quartiles and other relevant percentiles, minimum, and maximum, will be calculated for continuous variables. For discrete variables, descriptive analyses will be based on numbers of subjects and related percentages.

For binomially distributed variables, the treatment effect will be measured as differences in proportions. For continuous random variables, analysis of variance (ANOVA) or longitudinal models will be used for testing treatment differences or effects.

Statistical testing will be performed in a manner that maintains the family-wise Type I error rate at 0.05 and the coverage probability of confidence intervals at 95%. The sections of this SAP that describe each outcome provide details on the Type I error rate.

All data listings, summaries, and statistical analyses will be generated using SAS® Version 9.4 or higher or other validated software.

# 10. Handling Multiplicity of the Efficacy Analyses

This study, which investigates two doses of conbercept, has two primary hypotheses (one for each dose) and 10 secondary hypotheses (five for each dose).

The study has three time points of measurement associated with the primary and secondary hypotheses (36 weeks for the primary hypotheses and three of the secondary hypotheses for each dose; 48 weeks and 96 weeks for one secondary hypothesis for each dose). A Bonferroni correction will be used to handle multiplicity of the two primary non-inferiority hypotheses.

Each secondary outcome will be tested for non-inferiority of a conbercept dose against aflibercept using a Bonferroni correction that accounts for the five secondary outcomes and the two doses. Only if a dose shows non-inferiority for the primary outcome will the secondary outcomes for that dose be tested.

# 11. Primary Efficacy Analysis

As described in Section 2.2, the primary efficacy outcome is the mean change in BCVA letter score from Baseline to Week 36. The study has two non-inferiority primary hypotheses, one for each dose. Specifically, each conbercept dose will be evaluated against aflibercept with a non-inferiority margin for the mean of -4 letters.

# 12.1. Ocular and Non-ocular adverse events (AEs)

An AE is defined as any untoward medical occurrence associated with the administration of the study medication in a subject, whether or not the event is considered drug-related. An AE can therefore be any unfavorable and unintended sign (e.g., an abnormal laboratory finding), symptom, or disease occurring after the subject started dosing with the study drug, regardless of the causality. Any pre-existing medical condition that worsens after administration of the study medication will also be considered a new AE.

Adverse events (both elicited and observed) will be monitored throughout the study and documented on the appropriate CRF. AEs will be classified as ocular or non-ocular and will be coded by MedDRA and summarized by system organ class (SOC), and preferred term (PT). Adverse events will be summarized by subject, not event. The Investigator will promptly review each AE for accuracy and completeness, and classify each AE according to its intensity, its relationship to study drug or injection procedure, and its seriousness.

All AE presentations will summarize treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) defined as adverse events with onset on or after randomization or onset prior to randomization but with worsened severity post-randomization. AEs without a severity rating that are reported post-randomization will be considered treatment-emergent.

The following presentations of treatment-emergent adverse events will be generated:

- Serious adverse events;
- All adverse events;
- Adverse events suspected to be related to the study medication;
- Adverse events suspected to be related to the injection procedure;
- Adverse events leading to study discontinuation.

Separate summaries of treatment-emergent adverse events will present number of events.

# 12.1.1. Severity

Severity of an AE is defined as a qualitative assessment of the degree of intensity of an AE as determined by the Investigator or reported to him/her by the subject. The assessment of severity is made irrespective of relationship to study treatment or seriousness of the event and should be evaluated according to the following scale:

- Mild: Event is noticeable to the subject but is easily tolerated and does not interfere with the subject’s daily activities.
- Moderate: Event is bothersome, possibly requiring additional therapy, and may interfere with the subject’s daily activities.
- Severe: Event is intolerable, necessitates additional therapy or alteration of therapy, and interferes with the subject’s daily activities.

# 12.1.2. Relationship to study medication and injection procedure

The Investigator should use the following two categories to classify the relationship of each AE to the study medication and/or to the injection procedure:

- Suspected: A reasonable possibility exists that the study medication or the injection procedure caused the AE.
- Not Suspected: A reasonable possibility does not exist that the study medication and/or the injection procedure caused the AE.

A suspected adverse reaction is any AE for which there is a reasonable possibility that the study medication caused the AE. “Reasonable possibility” means there is evidence to suggest a causal relationship between the study medication and the AE. Types of evidence that would suggest a causal relationship between the study medication and the AE include the following:

- Follow a reasonable temporal sequence following the time of administration of the dose of study medication or injection procedure
- Resolve or improve after discontinuation of study medication or injection procedure
- Reappear or worsen when dosing with study medication or injection procedure is resumed

# 12.2. Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)

An AE is considered serious if it results in any of the following outcomes:

- Death; including all deaths, even those that appear to be completely unrelated to study medication
- A life-threatening AE;

Note: An AE is considered “life-threatening” if, in the view of the Investigator, its occurrence places the subject at immediate risk of death at the time of the event. It does not include an AE that had it occurred in a more severe form, might have caused death.
- Inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization;

Notes: The term “inpatient hospitalization” refers to any inpatient admission for longer than 24 hours. For chronic or long-term inpatients, inpatient admission includes transfer within the hospital to an acute/intensive care inpatient unit. Inpatient hospitalization does not include: emergency room visits or other visits if less than 24 hours; outpatient/same-day/ambulatory procedures; observation/short stay units; rehabilitation facilities; hospice facilities; or nursing homes.

The term “prolongation of existing hospitalization” refers to any extension of an inpatient hospitalization beyond the stay anticipated or required for the reason for the initial admission as determined by the Investigator or treating physician.
- A persistent or significant incapacity leading to substantial disruption of the ability to conduct normal life functions;

Note: A serious AE specifically related to visual threat (one that would require either surgical or medical intervention to prevent permanent loss of vision, or an AE that would cause substantial loss of vision) would be interpreted as any impairment to the subject’s eyesight (e.g., hemorrhage, retinal detachment, central corneal ulcer or damage to the optic nerve).
- A congenital anomaly/birth defect.
- An important medical event that may not result in death, are not life-threatening or do not require hospitalization may be considered SAEs when, based upon appropriate medical judgment, they may jeopardize the subject and may require medical or surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes listed in this definition.

# 12.2.1. Expectedness

# Appendix 4.1. Primary efficacy outcome:

This section outlines the approaches planned to analyze the data for the primary efficacy outcome. The primary analysis will use a mixed model for repeated measure (MMRM) with adaptive trimming of the measurements missing at Week 36 as outlined in Permutt and Li [8].

The primary efficacy analysis is based on the difference between the mean changes from baseline at Week 36 in each conbercept dose compared to the aflibercept dose estimated using an MMRM approach to trimmed data. Separate MMRM analysis will be performed comparing each conbercept dose to aflibercept. Each hypothesis of non-inferiority will be tested using a 1-sided Type I error rate of 0.0125. Trimming the data involves removing the subjects with the worst outcomes, that is, those with the worst change from baseline to Week 36 in letters gained. All drop-outs are trimmed. That makes the implicit assumption that drop-outs are among the worst performers. We recognize that a few may drop out because of reasons other than worsening vision or failure to improve; however, trimming them will have at most a trivial impact on the

# Appendix 4. 4. Exploratory efficacy outcomes:

Each exploratory outcome and the analysis approach for each outcome are described below. To evaluate differences between the conbercept doses and aflibercept with respect to the following outcome measures in the study eye:
