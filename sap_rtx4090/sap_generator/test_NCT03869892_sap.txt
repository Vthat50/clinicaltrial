## Introduction

This Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) describes the planned statistical analyses for the clinical study evaluating Trifluridine/tipiracil hydrochloride (S95005) compared to Capecitabine in the first-line treatment setting for patients with unresectable metastatic colorectal cancer (adenocarcinoma of the colon or rectum).

### Study Overview

This is a randomized clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Trifluridine/tipiracil hydrochloride (S95005) versus Capecitabine in adult patients (≥18 years) with histologically confirmed adenocarcinoma of the colon or rectum who meet the following criteria:

- Not candidates for standard full dose combination chemotherapy with irinotecan or oxaliplatin
- Not candidates for curative resection of metastatic lesions
- No previous systemic anticancer therapy for unresectable metastatic colorectal cancer
- ECOG performance status ≤2
- RAS status must be available or determinable

### Primary Objective

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate Progression-free Survival (PFS), defined as the time elapsed between randomization and the date of radiological tumour progression (according to RECIST 1.1) or death from any cause.

The secondary objectives include evaluation of:

- Overall Survival (OS) with follow-up up to 8 years
- Overall response rate (ORR)

## Sensitivity Analyses

These analyses will assess the impact of analytical assumptions, censoring rules, and potential sources of bias on the primary conclusions.

### Sensitivity Analyses for the Primary Endpoint (PFS)

#### Analysis 1: Unstratified Analysis

An unstratified log-rank test will be performed to assess PFS without adjustment for stratification factors. This analysis will evaluate whether the treatment effect is consistent when stratification is not considered.

**Rationale:** To confirm that the primary analysis results are not unduly influenced by the stratification structure.

The primary PFS analysis will be repeated in the per-protocol population, which includes all randomized subjects who received at least one dose of study treatment and had no major protocol deviations that could affect efficacy assessment.

**Rationale:** To evaluate the treatment effect under conditions of protocol adherence.

## Multiplicity Adjustment

This study evaluates trifluridine/tipiracil hydrochloride (S95005) versus capecitabine as first-line treatment in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer who are not candidates for standard full-dose combination chemotherapy.

### Hierarchical Testing Procedure

To control the overall Type I error rate at the two-sided α = 0.05 level across the primary and key secondary endpoints, a fixed-sequence (hierarchical) testing procedure will be employed. The endpoints will be tested in the following pre-specified order:

1. **Primary Endpoint**: Progression-free Survival (PFS)
2. **Key Secondary Endpoint**: Overall Survival (OS)
3. **Key Secondary Endpoint**: Overall Response Rate (ORR)

Under this hierarchical approach, each endpoint will be tested at the full two-sided α = 0.05 significance level, but testing of subsequent endpoints in the hierarchy is conditional upon achieving statistical significance for all preceding endpoints. Specifically:

- PFS will be tested first at α = 0.05 (two-sided)
- If and only if PFS achieves statistical significance (p < 0.05), OS will be tested at α = 0.05 (two-sided)
- If and only if both PFS and OS achieve statistical significance, ORR will be tested at α = 0.05 (two-sided)

If any endpoint in the sequence fails to achieve statistical significance, all subsequent endpoints in the hierarchy will be considered exploratory, and their p-values will be reported for descriptive purposes only without claims of statistical significance.

### Interim Analysis Considerations

No interim analyses are planned for this study. Therefore, no alpha spending function or adjustment for interim looks is required.

### Additional Analyses

Analyses of endpoints not included in the hierarchical testing procedure, as well as subgroup analyses, will be considered exploratory and will not be adjusted for multiplicity. Results from these analyses will be interpreted with appropriate caution given the potential for inflated Type I error.
