Introduction
2.1 Preface
Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) and Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) are highly
prevalent mental health disorders associated with poor quality of life, psychosocial
disability, reduced productivity, and increased medical utilization.[1, 2, 3, 4, 7] Although
categorized separately in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders
(DSM-5), Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) and Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
include a high degree of overlap. MDD is a recurrent mood disorder classified by a
two-week duration of least five symptoms including sadness, and/or loss of interest in
daily activities, as well as changes in weight or appetite, sleep disturbance, psychomotor
agitation/retardation, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness, and
thoughts of death/suicide.[8] MDD carries a lifetime risk of approximately 15% in the
overall population and up to 25% in women.[9] GAD is an anxiety disorder characterized
by persistent and excessive worry about a number of life areas (e.g., money, work,
health, family) lasting for at least 6 months and accompanied by symptoms such as
restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, and sleep
disturbance. Approximately 4% of the US population is affected by GAD,[2] which
equates to over 13 million people. Although categorized separately in the Diagnostic and
Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5), MDD and GAD frequently co-occur and
include a high degree of overlap. [5]
Theories for comorbidity include shared genetic risk [10, 11, 12, 13]as well as common
dysfunctional processes (for example, avoidance of situations and emotions; tendency to
engage in repetitive negative thinking; overestimation of the likelihood of negative
outcomes).[6] A focus on transdiagnostic processes may help to address overlap and
comorbidity between these disorders by addressing the processes commonly associated
with both MDD and GAD. Transdiagnostic treatments are based on interventions that
work across two or more disorders by targeting underlying processes that are common
to both. MDD and GAD represent a particularly appropriate target for a transdiagnostic
intervention due to significant overlap in symptoms, several commonalities in effective
treatments, and very high rates of comorbidity. [6, 7] Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy
(CBT) is used in the treatment of both MDD and GAD to target these dysfunctional
processes. CBT is the term used for a group of psychological treatments that are based
on scientific evidence and focus on changing unhelpful thoughts and behaviors to
address emotional disturbance. These therapies are usually short- term treatments that
focus on teaching patients specific skills. Treatments for MDD and GAD each include
some unique intervention components (for example, treatments for GAD may include
specific interventions to manage uncontrolled worry), however, many intervention targets
are shared across disorders (for example, a focus on challenging unhelpful thoughts,
reducing avoidance, and increasing engagement in valued behaviors). CBT interventions
common to both MDD and GAD may include:
1. Psychoeducation, which provides important psychological information
to the patient about the disorder(s) and processes being treated and
the techniques that will be used within the treatment.
2. Daily monitoring, which involves daily recordings of important symptoms or
behaviors. This is designed to gather information about the patient’s
symptom patterns (e.g., triggers, associated thoughts and behaviors),
as well as to increase the patient’s awareness of when they are
engaging in unhelpful behaviors or thought patterns.
3. Cognitive techniques, which emphasize making changes to
maladaptive thinking patterns that maintain psychiatric conditions.
Cognitive techniques might include: evaluating thoughts based on
evidence, conducting experiments to gather new evidence to evaluate
a thought, and thinking through the probability of negative outcomes.
4. Behavioral techniques, which are grounded in learning theory
(operant and/or classical conditioning) and emphasize reward,
punishment, habituation and extinction. Examples include exposure to
avoided stimuli or sensations to reduce anxiety, or engagement in
valued activities to increase exposure to rewards and improve mood.
For a full list of CBT interventions utilized in the HPDT-DA-013 treatment, see
Protocol Appendix A. Modality Labels for Interventions in HPDT-GAD-013 located in
the study protocol
In addition to CBT, the CBT-related disciplines of Acceptance and Commitment
Therapy (ACT) and Positive Psychology (PP) have been shown to be effective in
the treatment of MDD and GAD. [14, 15, 16] ACT is a form of psychotherapy that
emphasizes interventions that use acceptance and mindfulness strategies while
also emphasizing commitment to values and behavior-change strategies, to
increase psychological flexibility. Positive Psychology is the scientific study of the
positive aspects of the human experience that make life worth living.
HPDT-DA-013 is an 8-10-week prescription digital therapeutic (computerized
behavioral therapy) that delivers a transdiagnostic, personalized treatment for MDD
or GAD. The treatment in HPDT-DA-013 is primarily based on empirically supported
interventions from CBT, and enriched with ACT and PP. HPDT-DA-013 delivers
therapy in a sequence of modules of interventions, patient education, and
skill-building. It consists of text, video, quizzes, and a conversational Artificial
Intelligence (Al) chatbot, Anna. The therapy is delivered via a software application
intended to be used on a patient’s mobile device or a web-based portal. Patients
are recommended to finish treatment within 8-weeks; however, the product’s
design allows patients to complete their treatment in 10-weeks to allow for missed
interventions. The prescription will end automatically 10-weeks after the
participant’s activation date, defined as the date they begin their first intervention,
or after the participant completes their final activity, which may occur as early as
8-weeks after the participant’s activation date. The commercial name for
HPDT-DA-013 is Ensemble.
2.2
Purpose of the Analysis
The purpose of this trial is to determine the efficacy and safety of
HPDT-DA-013 Digital Therapeutic as a prescription-only digital therapeutic for
the treatment of MDD or GAD in participants 22 years of age and older.
3
Study Objectives and Endpoints
3.1 Study Objectives
The primary objectives of this study are to assess the efficacy and safety of
HPDT-DA-013 when given adjunctively to outpatient standard of care as a treatment
for MDD or GAD in adults, measured by change in PHQ-9 and GAD-7 total scores.
3.2
Endpoints
The primary and secondary efficacy and safety endpoints and success criteria are
listed below. Full descriptions of the analysis for each endpoint are provided in
sections 8 and 9.
3.2.1 Primary Efficacy Endpoints
1. The change in PHQ-9 total score from baseline to end of treatment (8-10 weeks) in
patients with MDD.
a.
The statistical success criterion for this endpoint will be met if there is a
statistically significant reduction in PHQ-9 total score from baseline to end of
treatment.
b. The clinical success criterion for this endpoint will be met if at least 40% of
patients in the MDD diagnostic group experience a reduction of 5 or more
points in PHQ-9 total score from baseline to end of treatment.
2. The change in GAD-7 total score from baseline to end of treatment (8-10 weeks) in
patients with GAD.
a. The statistical success criterion for this endpoint will be met if there is a
statistically significant reduction in GAD-7 total score from baseline to end of
treatment.
b. The clinical success criterion for this endpoint will be met if at least 40% of
patients in the GAD diagnostic group experience a reduction of 4 or more
points in GAD-7 total score from baseline to end of treatment.
3.2.2 Secondary Efficacy Endpoints
1. The change in PHQ-9 scale score from baseline to follow up time points of 1-
month, 3-months, 6-months, 9-months and 12-months post treatment in
patients with MDD
2. The change in GAD-7 scale score from baseline to follow up time points of 1-
month, 3-months, 6-months, 9-months and 12-months post treatment in
patients with GAD
3.2.3 Exploratory Endpoints
1. The change in quality of life with SF-36 and EQ-5D-5L scores from baseline to
end of treatment, 1-month, 3-months, 6-months, 9-months and 12-months post
treatment in patients with MDD or GAD
2. The change in healthcare utilization from baseline to end of treatment, follow
up time points of 3-months, 6-months, 9-months and 12-months post treatment
in patients with GAD or MDD
3. The change in WPAI scores from baseline to end of treatment, follow up time
points of 1- month, 3-months, 6-months, 9-months and 12-months post treatment
in patients with GAD or MDD
3.2.4 Safety Endpoints
1. The significant worsening of depression symptoms (PHQ-9 score increase of 5 or more
points) from baseline to end of treatment.
2. The significant worsening of anxiety symptoms (GAD-7 score increase of 5 or more points)
from baseline to end of treatment.
3. The incidence of suicidality as indicated by an increase from 0, 1, or 2 on item 9 of the
PHQ-9 to a 3 from baseline to end of treatment.

4 Study Methods
4.1
Study Design and Plan
This is a Real-World Evidence, single-arm, open-label study evaluating the safety and
efficacy of
HPDT-DA-013 digital therapeutic in adults ages 22 and older diagnosed
with MDD or GAD. The study will evaluate change in PHQ-9 scores, a measure of
depressive symptoms, and GAD-7 scores, a measure of anxiety symptoms, from
baseline to the end of the 8–10-week treatment, as the co-primary endpoints.
Secondary endpoints for this study include: Proportion of responders on PHQ-9
defined as improvement of 5 or more points in PHQ-9 total score from baseline to end
of treatment in patients with MDD, proportion of responders on GAD-7 defined as
improvement of 4 or more points in GAD-7 total score from baseline to end of
treatment in patients with GAD, and change in PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scale scores at
follow-up time points of 1-, 3-, 6-, 9- and 12-months post treatment period. This
study will also examine the safety of HPDT-DA-013 through the collection of adverse
events.
The HPDT-DA-013 product was designed to provide a flexible, evidence-based, and
widely available treatment option for adults with MDD or GAD. It is a non-medication
option based on principles of CBT, ACT, and Positive Psychology, intended to be
prescribed in an outpatient setting, adjunctive to standard of care. In addition, this
study was designed to ensure that the digital therapeutic is used in its intended
setting, namely the patient’s home or other convenient location. Thus, the study
design is fully decentralized with no scheduled onsite visits. A virtual platform allows
study participants additional access to care and reduces the cost of treatment. All
study data is self-reported.
This study is a single-arm, open-label study. All study participants will receive
HPDT-DA-013 treatment.
HPDT-DA-013 is intended to improve symptoms of MDD or GAD by providing
neurobehavioral interventions, to adults 22 years of age and older with MDD or
GAD. HPDT-DA-013 delivers interventions based primarily on CBT, and enriched with
ACT and PP, evidence-based treatments for MDD and GAD. Study participants will
be prescribed a dose of 2 interventions every day for 8-10 weeks. Interventions are
presented in a variety of formats including 1) conversations powered by the AI
chatbot Anna, 2) audio recordings (for education and guided relaxation), 3) video
recordings that demonstrate specific exercises, 4) written explanations of suggested
interventions and 5) quizzes that provide psychoeducation and reinforce key topics.

4.2
Inclusion-Exclusion Criteria and Study Population
The study population is adults, ages 22 years and older, with MDD or GAD diagnosis in
the absence of psychotic features. Study participants will be enrolled in strict adherence
with the inclusion and exclusion criteria of the study. Both female and male participants
will be recruited. The scientific integrity of the data will not be compromised by either
incomplete or over- representation of any racial or ethnic group.
No vulnerable subjects are planned to be enrolled in this study. This clinical
investigation will not include any participants that are cognitively impaired, as defined
by 45 CFR 46(a): Basic HHS Policy for Protection of Human Research Subjects.
The inclusion criteria are as follows:
1. Adults 22 years or older at the time of screening
2. Able to read, understand, and provide written, dated informed
consent prior to screening procedures
3. Meets DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for Major Depressive Disorder or
Generalized Anxiety Disorder as diagnosed by prescribing clinician
4. If receiving medication treatment for depressive or anxiety symptoms,
the dose has been stable for at least 12 weeks at the time of informed
consent
5. If currently receiving Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), has been in
treatment for at least 3 months at the time of informed consent
6. Ability to follow written and verbal instructions (English) as assessed by the
prescribing clinician
7.
Access to and willingness to use computer equipment/smartphone
compatible with the product platform over a functional network for the study
duration
8. Ability and willingness to comply with all testing and requirements
9. Currently residing in the United States
10. Able to provide contact information for a health care provider
The exclusion criteria are as follows:
1. Risk of suicide or has attempted suicide or has engaged in self-harm
behavior (e.g., cutting, burning) within 24 months of the screening visit as
assessed by prescribing clinician
2. Has had moderate to severe substance use disorders within the last 6 months
prior to Screening, except for disorders related to the use of caffeine or
nicotine
3. Current comorbid psychiatric diagnosis including but not limited to:
a.
Post-traumatic stress disorder
b. Psychotic disorder
c.
Schizophrenia
d. Bipolar I or II
e.
Anorexia Nervosa
f.
Borderline Personality Disorder
g. Traumatic brain injury
h. Dementia or other neurocognitive disorder
i.
Other symptomatic manifestations that in the opinion of the prescribing
clinician may confound study data/assessments
4. Current or past treatment with transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS)
5. Current or past treatment with electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)
6. Current or past use of ketamine including esketamine
7.
Post COVID-19 sequelae symptoms
8. Currently pregnant or planning to become pregnant during the treatment period
9. Current participation in another clinical study
10. Current or past use of Happify
4.3
Study Enrollment
This study is not randomized, as there is only one treatment arm.
Happify will use a social media marketing campaign to advertise for the product and
participation in the study. (See Protocol Appendix C: Marketing Campaign Materials,
located in the study protocol) These advertisements will connect interested individuals
to a landing page on the EmpiraMedTM PRO PortalTM Software Platform (the PRO Portal)
containing information about the HPDT-DA-013 study. (See Protocol Appendix D: Study
Landing Page, located in the study protocol)
Patients arrive at the study landing page and select a link to enter the PRO Portal, where
they will complete a self-registration intake form, create a user account, answer
eligibility questions, and sign an electronic informed consent. They will then be
connected with a Direct to Consumer (DTC) provider (MD) to schedule a telehealth
consult.
The provider will evaluate the patient and HPDT-DA-013 will be prescribed at the
discretion of the telemedicine provider. Participants are considered to be officially
enrolled in the study at the time point when the telemedicine provider dispenses the
investigational product and the participant creates an account in the Ensemble product.
(See Appendix E: Happify/EmpiraMed/Populus Patient Flow, located in the study
protocol)
Before any study specific procedures are conducted, the participant must sign and date a
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) authorization, a medical
release
form,
and
an electronic informed consent form (ICF) and acknowledge
EmpiraMed terms and conditions in the PRO Portal. Participants will be told that the
study is voluntary and is being conducted to assess the safety and efficacy of a digital
therapeutic (HPDT-DA-013) in the treatment of MDD or GAD adjunctive to standard of
care. They will also be informed of how their personal information will be secured and
that all analyses will be conducted on Coded Study Data. Participants will be able to
email or download an electronic copy of the consent form and it will also be available
from their Study Dashboard once they have completed the consent process. Participants
must be able to complete the eConsent process themselves; a legally authorized
representative (LAR) cannot consent the participant into the study. The ICF will include
contact information (email address and/or phone number) for the Sponsor, Principal
Investigator, and EmpiraMed so they can ask any questions, or if they would like to
withdraw from the study at any time.

5 Sample Size
The present study is a single-arm longitudinal observational study design, changes
PHQ-9 and GAD scores from pre- to post-intervention are of primary concern.
Estimated effect sizes from a previous similar study yielded a standardized mean
difference from pre- to post-intervention PHQ-9 of 0.74 (M = -4.67, SD = 6.3) and
GAD of .55 (M = -1.04, SD = 1.89). These reflect medium sized effects.
This study will use an expected recruited sample size of N=750, with approximately
50% of the sample being patients with MDD and approximately 50% being patients
with GAD. With an anticipated completion rate of 80%, approximately 300 patients in
each diagnostic group are expected to complete the study protocol.
For the MDD diagnostic group, a decrease of 5 points from baseline to end of
treatment in PHQ-9 total score will be considered the minimal clinically
important difference (MCID).
Assuming M = -5, SD = 6.3 and α = 0.05, 300 patients would achieve over 99%
statistical power using a one-sided matched-pairs t-test with a null hypothesis of no
mean change frombaseline and α = 0.05.
For the GAD diagnostic group, a decrease of 4 points from baseline to end of
treatment in GAD- 7 total score will be considered the MCID. Assuming M = -4, SD =
1.89 and α = 0.05, 300 patients would achieve over 99% statistical power using a
one-sided matched-pairs t-test with a null hypothesis of no mean change from
baseline and α = 0.05.
6 General Considerations
6.1
Timing of Analyses
Complete analysis of the efficacy and safety endpoints will be conducted once all
enrolled subjects have either completed the Week 8 visit, discontinued, or lost to
follow-up. Final analysis will be conducted upon study completion, following
database lock.
6.2
Analysis Populations
A total of three populations will be used for all summaries and analyses. Study
participants who have satisfied the population criteria will be classified in the
designated population and will only be included in analyses for which they have
available data.
Data analysis will be performed using the following analysis sets.
●
The Intent-to-Treat (ITT) population is defined as all study participants
who sign the informed consent, meet all screening criteria, are dispensed the
investigational product, are enrolled, and complete the end of treatment
(Week 8-10) PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scales. Enrollment is considered to have begun
at the moment that participant receives the email linking to the Ensemble
product. The ITT population will be used for all efficacy analyses.
●
The Safety Population (SAF) is defined as all study participants in the ITT
population who complete at least one intervention/activity in HPDT-DA-013.
The SAF population will be used for all safety analyses.
●
The Per-Protocol (PP) Population is defined as all study participants in
the SAF population who complete 90 interventions/activities and do not
experience any major protocol deviations that may impact the efficacy
assessments through the end of the treatment period. The PP population
will be used in sensitivity analyses of the primary endpoints.

6.4
Covariates and Subgroups
Unless otherwise specified, all planned analyses will be conducted separately for the
MDD diagnostic group and the GAD diagnostic group. Additionally, the data from
each group will be presented both separately and combined for all safety
summaries.
6.5
Missing Data
The primary efficacy analysis of this study compares baseline to end-of-treatment
scores on the PHQ-9 and GAD-7. At a minimum, patients must complete these
measures at baseline and at end of treatment (Week 8-10) in order for their data to
be included in the primary efficacy analysis. If the end of treatment (week 8-10)
assessment is missing, the patient’s score will be imputed for a sensitivity analysis
used to supplement the primary efficacy analysis.
Model-based multiple imputation using diagnostic group (MDD or GAD), baseline
demographic characteristics, number of interventions, and non-missing PHQ-9 or
GAD-7 values for that study participant will be used as predictors for the imputation.
This method will be detailed in section 8.1.3.
6.6
Interim Analyses and Data Monitoring
An analysis of collected data will be conducted once approximately 20% (N=150) of the
total projected study participants have completed the treatment period. The interim
analysis will include descriptive statistics (Section 7.4), the analysis of the primary
endpoints (Sections 8.1), exploratory efficacy analyses examining change in quality of
life from baseline to end of treatment (section 8.3.1), and the analysis of safety
endpoints described in section 9.1. Any other additional analyses planned for full
sample will not be included in the interim analysis. The full sample size is needed to
have sufficient statistical power for the exploratory analyses. The interim analysis will
be used to support a submission to a European regulatory body for CE medical device
approval.
6.7
Multi-Center Studies
This study will utilize one virtual clinical investigation site.
6.8
Multiple Testing
This study has two co-primary efficacy endpoints, each applying to a different
diagnostic group. As such, the study will be considered successful if either co-primary
endpoint is met. Since there is no overlap between the groups, each co-primary
endpoint will be evaluated using an alpha of 0.05.
The success of the secondary endpoints will be evaluated only if the corresponding
co-primary endpoint is met for that diagnostic group. There will be no Type I error
adjustment within the secondary endpoints.
There will be no Type I error adjustment for exploratory endpoints.
7 Summary of Study Data
All relevant data collected will be shown for each study participant in the
individual study participant data listings.
7.1
Data and Analysis Conventions
The following conventions will be used across all study summaries and
analyses, unless otherwise specified in the corresponding section.
●
Statistical summaries will include, at a minimum, sample size (N), mean,
median, standard deviation (SD), minimum (Min), maximum (Max) for
continuous and some ordinal variables; and sample size (N), frequencies
and percentages for categorical and some ordinal variables.
●
Statistical tests and confidence intervals will be two-sided unless otherwise
specified; statistical tests will use an alpha threshold of 0.05 and confidence
intervals will use 95% confidence unless otherwise specified. Test statistics,
p-values, degrees of freedom, and corresponding effect sizes (e.g., Cohen’s d
with corresponding 95% confidence interval) will be included for all statistical
tests.
●
Mixed Models for Repeated Measured (MMRM) will be fit using unstructured
covariance matrices. In the event of lack of convergence in the model fit,
other covariance structures (e.g. AR(1)) may be used.
●
P-values will be calculated to three decimals places. P-values that are less
than 0.001 will be presented as “<0.001.”
●
Partial dates will be imputed for safety data, using the most conservative
approach. Dates for safety data will be imputed to result in the longest
possible duration for adverse events and concomitant medication use.

8 Efficacy Analyses
8.1
Primary Efficacy Analyses
The ITT population will be used for the primary analysis of the co-primary endpoints.
8.1.1
Change in PHQ-9 total score from baseline to end of treatment (8-10
weeks) in patients with MDD
For the primary analysis of primary endpoint #1, a paired t-test will be used to
evaluate the statistical significance of whether the mean change-from-baseline
instrument score (PHQ-9 total score) at the end of treatment (week 8-10)
assessment is different from 0. The statistical test will be structured as
H0: µP = 0
vs.
H1: µP < 0
where µP is the mean within-subject change in PHQ-9 total score (End of
Treatment – Baseline). The success criterion will be met if the null hypothesis
is rejected at the 0.05 level.
For the clinical significance, the percent of responders (those meeting or
exceeding MCID for that instrument) will be evaluated. Within the MDD group,
a responder will be defined as a patient who experiences a change in PHQ-9
total score less than or equal to -5 (MCID) from baseline to end of treatment.
The success criterion will be met if at least 40% of patients in the MDD group
are responders.
8.1.2
Change in GAD-7 total score from baseline to end of treatment (8-10
weeks) in patients with GAD
For the primary analysis of primary endpoint #2, a paired t-test will be used to
evaluate the statistical significance of whether the mean change-from-baseline
instrument

H0: µG = 0
vs.
H1: µG < 0
where µG is the mean within-subject change in GAD-7 total score (End of
treatment – Baseline). The success criterion will be met if the null hypothesis is
rejected at the 0.05 level.
For the clinical significance, the percent of responders (those meeting or
exceeding MCID for that instrument) will be evaluated. Within the GAD group, a
responder will be defined as a patient who experiences a change in GAD-7 total
score less than or equal to -4 (MCID) from baseline to end of treatment. The
success criterion will be met if at least 40% of patients in the GAD group are
responders.

8.2
Secondary Efficacy Analyses
The ITT population will be used for the primary analysis of the secondary endpoints.
8.2.1
Change in PHQ-9 scale score from baseline to follow up time points
in patients with MDD
A MMRM will be fit for each diagnostic group across all time points using change
from baseline PHQ-9 total score as the response variable; time point as a fixed
effect; and a random effect for subject. A test of least squares means at each

8.2.2
Change in GAD-7 scale score from baseline to follow up time points
in patients with GAD
A MMRM will be fit for each diagnostic group across all time points using change
from baseline GAD-7 total score as the response variable; time point as a fixed
effect; and a random effect for subject. A test of least squares means at each
time point, with 0 as the null hypothesis and an alpha of 0.05, will be used to
evaluate this endpoint. The results will be provided in a table.

9 Safety Analyses
All safety analyses will be conducted using the SAF Population. Safety data will be
analyzed for each diagnostic group, as well as both groups combined.
9.1
Safety Endpoints
9.1.1
Incidence of significant worsening of depression symptoms
(PHQ-9 score increase of 5 or more points) between baseline
and end of treatment
The frequency and percentage of subjects in each diagnostic group, and both
groups combined, who experience an increase of 5 or more points in PHQ-9 total
score will be summarized by visit and presented in a table.
9.1.2
Incidence of significant worsening of anxiety symptoms (GAD-7 score
increase of 5 or more points) between baseline and end of treatment

9.2
Adverse Events
Adverse events (AEs) will be MedDRA (version 23.1) coded and characterized by

summarized by treatment overall, by severity, and by relationship to study

also be presented.
Worsening depression or anxiety will be monitored as an adverse event via
the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 respectively and suicidality will be monitored via item
9 on the PHQ-9.
