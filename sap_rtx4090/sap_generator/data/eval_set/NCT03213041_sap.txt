ObjectivesEvaluate the  impact on progression-free survival (PFS) with the combination 
carboplatin - pembrolizumab in patients with CTC positive, triple negative (TN) 
MBC who have not received prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease or 
have developed recurrence after completion of neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy 
or are de-novo stage IV
Number of Subjects up to 40  patients (32 evaluable)
Diagnosis and  Main 
Inclusion CriteriaMetastatic  TNBC who have not received prior chemotherapy for metastatic 
disease or have developed recurrence after completion of 
neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy or are de novo stage IV
CTC positive  (defined as CTC≥5)
Study designThis is  a prospective, single arm, Phase II combination study
Treatment PlanPatients will  be treated with pembrolizumab 200mg intravenously (IV) and 
carboplatin AUC 5 IV on day 1 of each cycle (1 cycle = 21 days).  Treatments 
continue until patient completes 24 months of treatment or 35 administrations 
of study medication, whichever is later, or until progressive disease or 
unacceptable toxicity. Patients will return for a follow up visit within 30 days of 
last treatment dose and will be followed and assessed every 9 weeks for 1 
year and after 1 year every 12 weeks to monitor disease status.  Patients will 
be contacted for survival status every 12 weeks until death, withdrawal of 
consent, or the end of study, whichever occurs first.
Statistical methodsThe trial  is a Phase II, prospective study testing the combination of 
pembrolizumab and carboplatin in patients with HER-2 negative, CTC positive 
(defined as CTCs≥5) MBC. The primary hypothesis to be tested is that the 
combination of Carboplatin intravenously and pembrolizumab will increase 
PFS in patients with ≥ 5 CTCs compared with the historical median of 4.9 
months [1].  The distribution of PFS will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier 
(KM) method.  Median survival and an associated 95% confidence interval will 
be estimated from the KM analysis.  The null hypothesis of no improvement in 
survival will be rejected if the lower bound of the confidence interval for median 
survival is greater than 4.9.

1.1 Circulating tumor  cells (CTCs) in Metastatic breast Cancer.
Breast cancer  mortality has been decreasing considerably over the past two decades as a result 
of earlier diagnosis and major treatment advances in the adjuvant and metastatic settings [2]. 
Despite this progress, metastatic disease is still largely considered an incurable condition and 5-
year survival rates are less than 25% [2]. Thus, the major treatment objectives in the advanced 
stage disease remain palliation of symptoms and improvement of quality of life. Importantly, 
metastatic breast cancer (MBC) is a heterogeneous disease and long-term patient outcome can 
be influenced by various biological features, as well as by the extent and site of metastatic 
disease. Typically, widespread visceral disease is associated with symptomatic condition leading 
to deterioration of the performance status and short survival [3]. Various molecular markers and 
blood-based tests have been investigated as surrogate for more aggressive disease, among 
them the most reliable appears enumeration of CTCs [4, 5].
During the  last decade, several techniques capable of detecting and quantifying CTCs in cancer 
patients have been developed. It has been proposed that subpopulation of CTCs with tumor 
initiating potential act as a central mediator of metastatic dissemination, giving rise to the 
formation of distant micrometastases, which subsequently generate overt detectable and 
frequently measurable lesions [6]. In support of this theory, multiple studies have shown that a 
number of CTCs higher or equal than 5 per 7.5 mL of blood, evaluated before starting systemic 
treatment, is associated with poor outcome in patients with MBC [7, 8]. In addition, high CTC 
counts are associated with greater metastatic tumor burden, expressed as number of metastatic 
sites [8, 9]. Importantly, despite this association, the prognostic value of CTCs is independent 
from the initial number of metastatic sites [8, 9]. Moreover, a recent pooled analysis of 1944 
patients with MBC was performed confirming those data [10]. Moreover, the authors created a 
clinicopathological prognostic model to determine the added impact of CTCs for progression free 
survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). They found that adding CTC count (<5 or ≥5) to their 
predictive model significantly increased the prognostication for OS and PFS. This is in contrast to 
serum tumor markers, CEA and CA15-3, when checked at 3-5 weeks or 6-8 weeks which did not 
add significant prognostic value in their model.  The prognostic value of CTCs was consistent 
across all subtypes of disease. Finally, in the multivariate analysis, CTC count was the strongest 
prognosticator for PFS and OS. Furthermore, continued elevation of CTCs is known to be 
associated with a poor prognosis as well as a sign of chemo-resistance. The prospective 
SWOGS0500 was designed to test the hypothesis that an early change in chemotherapy regimen 
in patients with positive CTCs (defined as ≥ 5 CTCs) after first cycle chemotherapy administered 
for previously untreated disease. A recent trial of MBC patients used persistently elevated CTCs 
as a marker of chemo-resistance and evaluated an early intervention of changing chemotherapy 
after one cycle to see if this would improve survival [1]. From October 1, 2006 until March 15, 
2012, a total of 595 eligible and evaluable patients were enrolled in the study and started on first 
line therapy. Approximately 330 patients were classified as HR+/Her-2 negative (Luminal 
subtype) and 134 patients had triple negative breast cancer (TNBC). All patients received first-
line chemotherapy. At baseline 276 patients (46%) had positive CTCs and 319 (54%) did not 
(arm A). Patients with HR+ disease had higher percentage of CTC positivity compared to TNBC 
(64 % vs. 21 % respectively). Of those patients with initially increased CTCs, 31 (10%) were not 
retested, 165 were evaluated at 3-4 weeks after chemotherapy and found to have normal (< 5 
CTCs) and assigned to continuation of treatment (arm B), and the 123 patients with persistent 
CTC elevation were randomly assigned to either maintain same therapy (arm C1) or change 
therapy (arm C2). Interestingly, at the second time point the percentage of CTCs ≥ 5 in HR+ 
disease increased (72% vs. 22%). No difference in median OS was observed between arm C1 
and C2 (10.7 and 12.5 months, respectively; P = .98). CTCs were strongly prognostic. Median 
OS for arms A, B, and C (C1 and C2 combined) were 35 months, 23 months, and 13 months, 
respectively (P < .001).  The authors concluded that, those patients with continually elevated 
CTCs, irrespective of their subtype status, obtained limited therapeutic benefit from continuation 
or change of chemotherapy regimen. These patients had a shortened OS, and there was no

reason for treatment failure and metastasis development in patients with ERα-positive tumors.

1.2 Pharmaceutical  and Therapeutic Background
The importance  of intact immune surveillance in controlling outgrowth of neoplastic 
transformation has been known for decades.  Accumulating evidence shows a correlation 
between tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in cancer tissue and favorable prognosis in various 
malignancies.  In particular, the presence of CD8+ T-cells and the ratio of CD8+ effector T-cells / 
FoxP3+ regulatory T-cells seems to correlate with improved prognosis and long-term survival in 
many solid tumors.
The PD-1  receptor-ligand interaction is a major pathway hijacked by tumors to suppress immune 
control.  The normal function of PD-1, expressed on the cell surface of activated T-cells under 
healthy conditions, is to down-modulate unwanted or excessive immune responses, including 
autoimmune reactions.  PD-1 (encoded by the gene Pdcd1) is an Ig superfamily member related 
to CD28 and CTLA-4 which has been shown to negatively regulate antigen receptor signaling 
upon engagement of its ligands (PD-L1 and/or PD-L2).  The structure of murine PD-1 has been 
resolved.  PD-1 and family members are type I transmembrane glycoproteins containing an Ig 
Variable-type (V-type) domain responsible for ligand binding and a cytoplasmic tail which is 
responsible for the binding of signaling molecules.  The cytoplasmic tail of PD-1 contains 2 
tyrosine-based signaling motifs, an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibition motif (ITIM) and an 
immunoreceptor tyrosine-based switch motif (ITSM).  Following T-cell stimulation, PD-1 recruits 
the tyrosine phosphatases SHP-1 and SHP-2 to the ITSM motif within its cytoplasmic tail, leading 
to the dephosphorylation of effector molecules such as CD3ζ, PKCθ  and ZAP70 which are 
involved in the CD3 T-cell signaling cascade.  The mechanism by which PD-1 down modulates T-
cell responses is similar to, but distinct from that of CTLA-4 as both molecules regulate an 
overlapping set of signaling proteins.  PD-1 was shown to be expressed on activated lymphocytes

has also been shown during thymic development on CD4-CD8- (double negative) T-cells as well 
as subsets of macrophages and dendritic cells.  The ligands for PD-1 (PD-L1 and PD-L2) are

with no known signaling motifs.  Binding of either PD-1 ligand to PD-1 inhibits T-cell activation 
triggered through the T-cell receptor.  PD-L1 is expressed at low levels on various non-

detectably expressed on antigen-presenting cells found in lymphoid tissue or chronic 
inflammatory environments.  PD-L2 is thought to control immune T-cell activation in lymphoid 
organs, whereas PD-L1 serves to dampen unwarranted T-cell function in peripheral tissues.  
Although healthy organs express little (if any) PD-L1, a variety of cancers were demonstrated to 
express abundant levels of this T-cell inhibitor.  PD-1 has been suggested to regulate tumor-
specific T-cell expansion in subjects with melanoma.  This suggests that the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway 
plays a critical role in tumor immune evasion and should be considered as an attractive target for 
therapeutic intervention.
Pembrolizumab is  a potent and highly selective humanized monoclonal antibody (mAb) of the 
IgG4/kappa isotype designed to directly block the interaction between PD-1 and its ligands, PD-
L1 and PD-L2.  Pembrolizumab (KeytrudaTM) has recently been approved in the United States for 
the treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma and disease progression 
following ipilumumab and, if BRAF V600 mutation positive, a BRAF inhibitor.

Immune-Mediated Pneumonitis
 Immune-mediated  pneumonitis, including fatal cases, occurred in patients receiving 
pembrolizumab. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis. Evaluate

2) pneumonitis, and permanently discontinue pembrolizumab for severe (Grade 3), life-
threatening (Grade 4), or recurrent moderate (Grade 2) pneumonitis. Melanoma 
Pneumonitis occurred in 32 (2.0%) of 1567 patients receiving pembrolizumab in Trials 1, 
2, and 6, including Grade 1 (0.8%), Grade 2 (0.8%), and Grade 3 (0.4%) pneumonitis.

1.2.2 Rationale  for Dose Selection/Regimen/Modification 
Pembrolizumab
An open-label Phase  I trial (Protocol 001) is being conducted to evaluate the safety and 
clinical activity of single agent pembrolizumab.  The dose escalation portion of this trial 
evaluated three dose levels, 1 mg/kg, 3 mg/kg, and 10 mg/kg, administered every 2 
weeks (Q2W) in subjects with advanced solid tumors.  All three dose levels were well 
tolerated and no dose-limiting toxicities were observed.  This first in human study of 
pembrolizumab showed evidence of target engagement and objective evidence of tumor 
size reduction at all dose levels (1 mg/kg, 3 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg Q2W).  No maximum 
tolerated dose (MTD) has been identified to date.  Recent data from other clinical studies 
within the pembrolizumab program has shown that a lower dose of pembrolizumab and a 
less frequent schedule may be sufficient for target engagement and clinical activity.
Pharmacokinetic (PK)  data analysis of pembrolizumab administered Q2W and Q3W 
showed slow systemic clearance, limited volume of distribution, and a long half-life (refer 
to Investigator Brochure).  Pharmacodynamic data (IL-2 release assay) suggested that 
peripheral target engagement is durable (>21 days).  This early PK and 
pharmacodynamic data provides scientific rationale for testing a Q2W and Q3W dosing 
schedule.  
A population  PK analysis has been performed using serum concentration time data from 
476 patients. Within the resulting population PK model, clearance and volume 
parameters of pembrolizumab were found to be dependent on body weight. The 
relationship between clearance and body weight, with an allometric exponent of 0.59, is 
within the range observed for other antibodies and would support both body weight 
normalized dosing or a fixed dose across all body weights.  Pembrolizumab has been 
found to have a wide therapeutic range based on the melanoma indication.  The 
differences in exposure for a 200 mg fixed dose regimen relative to a 2 mg/kg Q3W body 
weight based regimen are anticipated to remain well within the established exposure 
margins of 0.5 – 5.0 for pembrolizumab in the melanoma indication. The exposure 
margins are based on the notion of similar efficacy and safety in melanoma at 10 mg/kg 
Q3W vs. the proposed dose regimen of 2 mg/kg Q3W (i.e. 5-fold higher dose and 
exposure). The population PK evaluation revealed that there was no significant impact of 
tumor burden on exposure. In addition, exposure was similar between the NSCLC and 
melanoma indications. Therefore, there are no anticipated changes in exposure between 
different indication settings.
The rationale for  further exploration of 2 mg/kg and comparable doses of pembrolizumab 
in solid tumors is based on: 1) similar efficacy and safety of pembrolizumab when dosed

Carboplatin
Patients included  in the study are TNBC with CTCs+. Those patients have been exposed 
to taxanes in the adjuvant or neoadjuvant setting. Furthermore, the prospective SWOG 
study evaluating the impact of chemotherapy change in patients with detectable CTCs at 
time of recurrence and known for the negative impact of this strategy confirmed previous 
observations [1]. Patients with CTCs do not benefit from single agent taxanes as first-line 
strategy and therefore, other agents should be selected for these patients. The 
combination of NGS and CTC detection and NGS suggests a correlation with higher 
mutational rate and particular association with TP53 and cell cycle associated genes. 
Platinum salts have demonstrated activity in MBC. A retrospective study by Sirohi et al. 
reviewed 155 patients with metastatic breast cancer (34 with TNBC) treated with 
platinum-based chemotherapy [19]. A little gain in progression-free survival (PFS) (6 vs. 4 
months, p = 0.05), but no significant differences in OS and overall response rate (41% vs. 
31%, p = 0.3) were observed in TNBC patients compared to non-TNBC.  The TBCRC009 
study demonstrated single agent activity of both cisplatin and carboplatin in first-line TN 
MBC [20].  The TNT trial is the first randomized phase III trial testing the activity of single-
agent platinum chemotherapy against a standard chemotherapy with single-agent taxane 
for TNBC patients or BRCA1/2 mutation carriers [21]. Three hundred seventy-six patients 
were enrolled in the study and randomly assigned to receive carboplatin or docetaxel for 
6–8 cycles, with crossover possibility on progression. After a median follow-up of 11 
months, there was no significant improvement in overall response rate for carboplatin 
over docetaxel (31.4% vs. 35.6%, respectively; p = 0.44). However, among patients 
carrying a germline BRCA1/2 mutation (n = 48), the objective response rate was more 
than two-fold higher when treated with carboplatin (68% vs. 33.3%, p = 0.03), with a 
longer PFS (6.8 vs. 3.1 months). In contrast, docetaxel had a median PFS of 4.7 months, 
with no differences according to BRCA1/2 mutational status.

1.3 Rationale  for Current Study
MBC is  a heterogeneous disease and long-term patient outcome can be influenced by various 
biological features, as well as by the extent and site of metastatic disease. Various molecular 
markers and blood-based tests have been investigated as surrogate for more aggressive 
disease, among them the most reliable appears enumeration of CTCs [4, 5].  Elevated baseline 
CTCs in MBC is an indication of aggressive disease with propensity to further progress with new 
lesions irrespective of disease subtype.  Moreover, we recently showed that patients with 
elevated CTC counts have greater tendency for an early progression and particularly with 
development of new sites and visceral metastases [12] suggesting that treatments with the ability 
to reduce or eliminate CTCs can have a significant impact on outcome.  CTCs express markers 
of immune suppression (e.g. PD-L1) and blockade of the programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) and its 
ligand (PD-L1) with pembrolizumab may result in improve disease control and outcome as 
measured by PFS and OS.

1.4.1 The  association of CTC detection with specific genomic abnormalities in metastatic tissue 
analyzed by next-genomic sequencing (NGS). 
We reasoned  that this approach will identify potential therapeutic targets and/or the level 
of genomic instability associated with CTC-positive disease. Studies have shown that 
tumors with increased number of mutations tend to have increased T-cell dysfunction or 
immune suppression and may benefit from immune therapy aimed at restoring T-cell 
function.
In fact,  cancer cells within the same tumor can exhibit different genotypes and the 
development of NGS methods helped to provide fundamental insight on the genetic intra-
tumor heterogeneity [23]. This clonal heterogeneity confers greater resistance to 
selective environmental pressures and, most of all, to systemic treatments [24]. 
Therefore, based on these preliminary considerations we decided to analyze the 
correlation between CTC enumeration and genomic alterations in tumor tissue of MBC 
patients.

genes known to be somatically altered in solid tumors. Patients with both tests were

from 7 MBC patients. HER2-positive tumors were excluded because of the lack of 
prediction of CTC count in disease treated with HER2-targeted therapy. Eight tissue 
samples from seven patients were sent for NGS which revealed 38 unique mutations with 
a total of 53 mutational events. The most common mutations/alterations were TP53

1.4.2 The  association of CTC detection with activation of innate immunity and T-cell 
dysfunction.
Previous studies have  demonstrated abnormal immune functions in patients with 
advanced solid tumors including breast cancer [25, 26]. In particular, 
CD4+CD25+FOXP3+ and CD8+CD28– Tregs are usually elevated in cancer patients.  
Chemotherapy was traditionally thought to be deleterious to anti-tumor immunity, but 
some chemotherapy agents may have the potential to augment anti-cancer immunity by 
inhibiting immune tolerance and suppression [27-30]. Paclitaxel has been found to inhibit 
the number and function of CD4+CD25+FOXP3+ Tregs directly [27]. In addition, 
cyclophosphamide was reported to inhibit the activity of CD4+CD25+ Tregs [8], and the 
generation and function of CD8+CD28– Tregs [28, 29]. In contrast, arsenic trioxide, 
dacarbazine, 5-fluorouracil and methotrexate increase the frequency of Tregs in 
peripheral blood mononuclear cells activated with T-cell mitogen [30]. The combination of 
gemcitabine and cisplatin demonstrated the ability to reduce CD4+CD25+FOXP3+ and 
CD8+CD28– Tregs at 3 weeks after administration of first dose [31]. The above data 
suggest that chemotherapeutic agents may have varying effects on Treg populations and 
activity. It has been postulated that combining some of these chemotherapeutic agents 
with immune-checkpoint regulators could decrease immune suppression and increase 
the efficacy of immune therapy [32]. Theoretically, the concomitant administration of 
chemotherapy and immune-therapy may not necessarily represent the optimal solution 
for achieving maximum benefit and alternative solutions should be explored to maximize 
synergistic effect. More recently it has been proposed that in patients with recurrent 
disease and reduced likelihood of response to chemotherapy an initial treatment with 
immune-therapy can “prime” the cancer cells to a more effective cytotoxic effect of 
chemotherapy. Therefore, patients with detectable CTCs would likely benefit from a 
“window” treatment with immune therapy before receiving combination regimen [33].  
Our group has been investigating  the possibility of identifying members of the innate 
immune system in the peripheral blood of patients with solid tumors to understand the 
complex relationship with cancer cells. We performed a prospective study using two 
different methods of selection for the identification of CTCs, the FDA-approved 
CellSearch® and a microfiltration system (CellSieve™). During this study we identified 
cancer-associated macrophage-like cells (CAMLs) that appear to indicate a more 
aggressive disease in metastatic conditions [34]. More recently, we demonstrated the 
expression of immune checkpoint pathways, including the programmed cell death 
protein-1 (PD-1) and the ligand B7-H1/PD-L1 [Figure 2].

4 TREATMENT PLAN
4.1 Treatment  Overview
This is  a phase II, prospective trial testing the combination of pembrolizumab and carboplatin in 
patients with HER-2 negative, CTCs positive (defined as CTCs≥5) MBC.  Patients will be treated 
with pembrolizumab 200mg intravenously (IV) on Day 1 of each cycle (1 cycle = 21 days) with 
carboplatin AUC 5 IV on Day 1 beginning at cycle 1.  Treatments continue until patient completes 
24 months of treatment 35 administrations of study medication, whichever is later, or until 
progressive disease or unacceptable toxicity.  The primary hypothesis will be that the combination 
of pembrolizumab IV and carboplatin IV will increase PFS in patients with ≥ 5 CTCs compared 
with the historical median of 4.9 months.

Subjects should  maintain a normal diet unless modifications are required to manage an AE such 
as diarrhea, nausea or vomiting.
Pembrolizumab may  have adverse effects on a fetus in utero.  Furthermore, it is not known if 
pembrolizumab has transient adverse effects on the composition of sperm.  Non-pregnant, non-
breast-feeding women may be enrolled if they are willing to use 2 methods of birth control or are 
considered highly unlikely to conceive.  Highly unlikely to conceive is defined as 1) surgically 
sterilized, or 2) postmenopausal (a woman who is ≥45 years of age and has not had menses for 
greater than 1 year will be considered postmenopausal), or 3) not heterosexually active for the 
duration of the study.  The two birth control methods can be either two barrier methods or a 
barrier method plus a hormonal method to prevent pregnancy. Subjects should start using birth 
control from study Visit 1 throughout the study period up to 120 days after the last dose of study 
therapy. 
The following  are considered adequate barrier methods of contraception: diaphragm, condom (by 
the partner), copper intrauterine device, sponge, or spermicide.  Appropriate hormonal 
contraceptives will include any registered and marketed contraceptive agent that contains an 
estrogen and/or a progestational agent (including oral, subcutaneous, intrauterine, or 
intramuscular agents).
Subjects should  be informed that taking the study medication may involve unknown risks to the 
fetus (unborn baby) if pregnancy were to occur during the study.  In order to participate in the 
study they must adhere to the contraception requirement (described above) for the duration of the 
study and during the follow-up period defined in section 8.4.2-Reporting of Pregnancy and 
Lactation to the Sponsor and to Merck.  If there is any question that a subject will not reliably 
comply with the requirements for contraception, that subject should not be entered into the study.
If a subject  inadvertently becomes pregnant while on treatment with pembrolizumab, the subject 
will immediately be removed from the study.  The site will contact the subject at least monthly and 
document the subject’s status until the pregnancy has been completed or terminated.  The 
outcome of the pregnancy will be reported to the PI and to Merck without delay and within 24 
hours to the Sponsor and within 2 working days to Merck if the outcome is a serious adverse 
experience (e.g., death, abortion, congenital anomaly, or other disabling or life-threatening 
complication to the mother or newborn).  
The study  investigator will make every effort to obtain permission to follow the outcome of the 
pregnancy and report the condition of the fetus or newborn to the Sponsor.   If a male subject 
impregnates his female partner the study personnel at the site must be informed immediately and 
the pregnancy reported to the Sponsor and to Merck and followed as described above and in 
Section 8.4.2.
It is  unknown whether pembrolizumab is excreted in human milk.  Since many drugs are excreted 
in human milk, and because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the nursing infant, 
subjects who are breast-feeding are not eligible for enrollment.

6.1 Summary
This study  is a prospective, single arm Phase II study with a primary endpoint PFS.  Median 
survival will be compared with a historical median survival of 4.9 months that is similar in first-line 
recurrence or de-novo stage IV TNBC.  Unless otherwise noted, all analysis will be performed 
using SAS 9.4 (SAS/STAT 13.2) or later.    
6.2 Sample  Size Justification
Sample size  calculations were performed using the method of Lawless as implemented in the 
SWOG online calculator (http://www.swogstat.org/stat/public/one_survival.htm).  The length of 
accrual was assumed to be 18 months followed by an additional 18 months of follow-up for a total 
study period of 3 years.  To have 90% power to detect an improvement in median PFS to 8.9 
months assuming a two-sided alpha of 0.05, this Phase II study requires 32 treated and evaluable 
subjects. Allowing for 10% dropout, we will treat a maximum of 38 subjects.   Assuming 35-40% 
of screened subjects will have ≥ 5 CTCs, we will screen approximately 100 subjects. 
6.3 Primary  Endpoint
To test  if the combination of Carboplatin intravenously and pembrolizumab will increase PFS in 
patients with ≥ 5 CTCs compared with the historical median of 4.9 months. 
PFS will  be based on investigator assessment. PFS will be defined as the time from the first study 
treatment to the first occurrence of progression or death, and will be assessed every 9 weeks 
after beginning of treatment.  
Any patient  who has completed at least 3 cycles of study treatment are evaluable for this 
endpoint.
6.4 Secondary  Endpoints
To  determine the toxicity and safety of the combination pembrolizumab-carboplatin in 
patients with CTC positive, HER2 negative MBC who have not received prior 
chemotherapy for metastatic disease or have developed recurrence after completion of 
neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy  or are de-novo stage IV. Adverse Events will be assessed 
according to the NCI CTCAE 4.03.
To  evaluate the impact on OS, ORR and CBR with combination Carboplatin - 
pembrolizumab in patients with CTCs positive MBC who have not received prior 
chemotherapy for metastatic disease or have developed recurrence after completion of 
neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy or are de-novo stage IV.  ORR and CBR will be evaluated 
according to RECIST criteria and in relation to PDL-1 expression in tissue and CTCs.  
To  assess immune-related response using tumor response by irRECIST as immune-
related PR (irPR) or CR (irCR). We will define immune-related clinical benefit rate as 
immune-related stable disease (irSD), irPR, or irCR.
Measure  the time to new metastases (TTNM).
Any patient  who has completed at least 3 cycles of study treatment are evaluable for this 
endpoint.
6.5 Exploratory  Endpoints
Measure immune  biomarkers (PDL-1) in CTCs (CellSearch™) and immune cells such as CAMLs 
(CellSieve™) and correlate with therapeutic benefit.

6.6 Statistical Analysis Plan
The primary  hypothesis to be tested in this Phase 2 study is that the combination of Carboplatin 
AUC 5 intravenously and pembrolizumab (Keytruda®) will increase progression-free survival in 
patients with ≥ 5 CTCs compared with the historical median of 4.9 months [1].  The distribution of 
progression-free survival will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.  Median survival and 
an associated 95% confidence interval will be estimated from the KM analysis.  The null 
hypothesis of no improvement in survival will be rejected if the lower bound of the confidence 
interval for median survival is greater than 4.9.  All available data from all eligible treated patients 
will be included in efficacy analyses.  Patients alive and not progressed at last follow-up will be 
censored at that time.  
Change in  CTCs longitudinally will be modeled using mixed effects regression.  If possible, we 
will transform CTCs so that mixed effects models for normally distributed outcomes may be used.  
If a suitable transformation cannot be found or if there is zero-inflation, linear mixed effects 
models for zero-inflated outcomes as implemented in Mplus software will be used.  
Analysis of  all time-to-event outcomes (OS, PFS, and TTNM) will begin with Kaplan-Meier plots 
and estimation of median survival times and associated 95% confidence intervals.  
Association with baseline  CTCs and other markers will be evaluated using log-rank tests with 
markers dichotomized at the median or other clinically relevant cut point.  Analysis of association 
of baseline markers adjusted for other clinical characteristics will be performed using Cox 
proportional hazards regression or accelerated failure-time models, as appropriate.  
Correlations among  markers for secondary endpoints will be estimated using Pearson or 
Spearman correlation coefficients.  
Prior to Amendment  2 (dated July 30, 2018), the first six patients who were treated with 
sequential pembrolizumab followed by combination therapy and included endocrine-refractory 
disease will be used only to assess safety.  This has been completed and there were no dose 
limiting toxicities in these patients.  As the protocol is written, 38 patients will be accrued in order 
to obtain 32 patients who have been treated and are evaluable for progression-free survival 
(PFS).  These numbers will be applied to the amended patient population.  The null hypothesis 
will still be rejected if there is evidence that the median PFS exceeds 4.9 months.

7.1 Pembrolizumab
The investigator  shall take responsibility for and shall take all steps to maintain

7.1.1 Other  names
KeytrudaTM;  MK-3475
7.1.2 Classification  – type of agent
Humanized X  PD-1_mAb (H409A11) IgG4
7.1.3 Mode  of action
Highly selective  anti-PD-1 humanized monoclonal antibody which inhibits programmed 
cell death-1 (PD-1) activity by binding to the PD-1 receptor on T-cells to block PD-1 
ligands (PD-L1 and PD-L2) from binding. Blocking the PD-1 pathway inhibits the negative 
immune regulation caused by PD-1 receptor signaling (Hamid, 2013). Anti-PD-1 
antibodies (including pembrolizumab) reverse T-cell suppression and induce antitumor 
responses.

Please refer  to pharmacy manual for detailed instructions

conditions specified on the label.
Store Pembrolizumab  vials under refrigeration at 2 to 8 degrees Celsius (0C) or 36 to 46 
degrees Fahrenheit (0F). Do not freeze; do not shake. 
Prior to reconstitution,  the vials of lyophilized powder can be out of refrigeration 
(temperatures at or below 25°C (77°F)) for up to 24 hours.
Storage following  reconstitution and dilution: Store at room temperature for a cumulative 
time up to 4 hours (this includes room temperature storage of the diluted infusion solution 
and the duration of infusion). IV bags and/or reconstituted vials can be also stored 
refrigerated at 2—8 degrees 0C (36—46 degrees 0F) for up to 20 hours.  If refrigerated, 
allow the vials and/or IV bags to come to room temperature prior to use. Do not freeze. 
Receipt and dispensing  of trial medication must be recorded by an authorized person at 
the trial site.
Clinical supplies  may not be used for any purpose other than that stated in the protocol

7.2.1 Other  names
CBDCA, Paraplatin,  JM-8, NSC-241240
7.2.2 Classification  – type of agent
Second-generation tetravalent  organic platinum compound
7.2.3 Mode  of Action
Like cisplatin,  carboplatin binds to DNA, thereby inhibiting DNA synthesis, in a cell cycle 
nonspecific manner. Carboplatin must first undergo activation to produce antineoplastic 
activity. Bidentate carboxylate ligands of carboplatin are displaced by water forming 
(aquation) positively charged platinum complexes which bind to nucleophilic sites in DNA, 
such as the O-6 position on guanine. Carboplatin produces predominantly interstrand 
DNA crosslinks rather than DNA-protein crosslinks. Intrastrand crosslinks result from the 
formation of adducts between the activated platinum complexes of the drug and the N-7 
atom (not exclusively) atom on guanine to produce 1, 2 intrastrand links between 
adjacent guanine molecules, between neighboring guanine and adenosine molecules, or 
between neighboring guanine molecules. Interstrand cross-linking within the DNA helix 
also occurs. Platinum adducts may inhibit DNA replication, transcription and ultimately 
cell division.
7.2.4 Storage  and stability
Intact vials are  stored at room temperature protected from light. The reconstituted 
solution is stable for at least 24 hours. When further diluted in glass or polyvinyl plastic to 
a concentration of 10mg/mL with normal saline or 5% dextrose carboplatin is stable for 8 
hours at 25 degrees C. Stability with further dilution to 0.5mg/mL has been reported for 
up to 8 hours. Other stability data indicate that carboplatin is stable for up to 24 hours and 
may be refrigerated, however, the manufacturer recommends that reconstituted solutions 
be discarded after 8 hours due to the lack of preservative in drug formulation.
7.2.5 Protocol  dose specifics
Carboplatin will  be given by IV at an area under the curve (AUC) dose of 5. Routine 
premedication should include at least a 5 – HT3 antagonist and dexamethasone. 
Carboplatin dose calculation is be per institutional standard and based on the serum 
creatinine of the day of visit.
7.2.6 Preparation
Add 5,  15, or 45 mL sterile water, normal saline, or 5% dextrose to the 50, 150, or 450 
mg vial, respectively. The resulting solution contains 10 mg/mL. The desired dose is 
further diluted, usually in 5% dextrose.
7.2.7 Route  of administration for this study
IV over 30  minutes.
7.2.8 Incompatibilities
Aluminum displaces  platinum from the carboplatin molecule, resulting in the formation of 
a black precipitate and loss of potency. Carboplatin solutions should not be prepared or 
administered with needles, syringes, catheters, or IV administration sets containing 
aluminum parts that might be in contact with the drug.
7.2.9 Availability  & Supply
Commercially available.
Concomitant myelosuppressive  drugs or radiation therapy may potentiate the 
hematologic toxicity of carboplatin. Concomitant nephrotoxic drugs may potentiate the 
nephrotoxicity of carboplatin, particularly when carboplatin is given in high-dose 
chemotherapy regimens.

9.3.1 CTCs  enumeration and PDL-1 evaluation
CTC isolation and  enumeration will be performed using the US Food and Drug

beads, and labels the cells with the fluorescent nucleic acid dye 4, 2-diamidino-2-

9.3.2 CTCs  and CAMLs using CellSieve™ 
The CellSieve  Microfiltration assay isolates CTCs and CAMLs by size exclusion, 
identifying CTCs or CAMLs by their morphological features and the phenotypic 
expression of CD14, cytokeratins 8, 18, and 19, CD45 and DAPI. The low-pressure 
vacuum system uses a specialized, holder attached to a syringe pump, to provide steady 
state flow of samples. Peripheral blood (7.5 mL) collected in CellSave preservative tubes 
is prefixed for 15 min, placed into the 30-mL syringe, and drawn through the filter in ∼3 
min. The filter is then washed, post-fixed, and permeabilized for 15 min. The filter and 
cells are stained with an antibody mixture and blocking buffer of FITC-conjugated anti-
cytokeratin 8, 18, 19, phycoerythrin (PE)-conjugated CD14, and Cy5-conjugated CD45 
(Creatv MicroTech, Inc.).

9.3.4 Human  and Mouse T-cell Receptor Sequencing
The cellular adaptive immune  system generates a remarkable breadth of diversity in 
antigen-specific T-cell receptors (TCR) by combinatorial recombination of gene segments 
in lymphocytes. The TCR is composed of two peptide chains, encoded by the T-cell 
receptor alpha and beta (TCRA, TCRB) or T-cell receptor gamma and delta (TCRG, 
TCRB) genes. There are thus two types of T-cell receptors, αβ and γδ, that differ by the 
TCR heterodimer type and immune function. The antigenic specificity of T-lymphocytes is 
in large part determined by the amino acid sequence in the hypervariable 
complementarity-determining region 3 (CDR3) of T-cell receptors. The existence of 
multiple V and J gene segments at the T-cell loci permits a large combinatorial diversity 
in receptor composition; and the non-templated insertion or deletion of nucleotides at the 
V-J, V-D, and D-J junctions further adds to the potential diversity of receptors that can be 
encoded. Because of the potential diversity of receptors (a healthy adult has 
approximately 10 million different TCRB chains contained within their 10 [39, 40] 
circulating T-cells[39]) it is highly improbable to randomly converge on the same TCRB 
nucleotide CDR3 sequence, effectively making each CDR3 sequence a unique tag for a 
T-cell clone.
Adaptive Biotechnologies’  immunoSEQ Assay, a multiplex PCR-based method that 
amplifies rearranged TCR CDR3 sequences (Fig. 6) and exploits the capacity of high 
throughput sequencing technology, characterizes tens of thousands of TCRB CDR3 
chains simultaneously. The primary obstacle to addressing amplification bias in repertoire 
analysis has been the lack of any gold standard in which the exact concentration of each 
target is known prior to amplification. To address this problem, Adaptive Biotechnologies 
developed a rigorous PCR amplification bias-control process, ensuring a quantitative 
read-out of the adaptive immune repertoire2. Additionally, because the technology 
utilizes genomic DNA, the frequency of sequenced CDR3 chains is highly representative 
of the relative frequency of each CDR3 sequence in the sample population of T-cells. 
Thus, the assay captures the full TCR repertoire including specific individual clones. The 
immunoSEQ Assay provides a novel method to identify and track the presence and 
frequency of common and rare clones in the context of the total adaptive immune system.
