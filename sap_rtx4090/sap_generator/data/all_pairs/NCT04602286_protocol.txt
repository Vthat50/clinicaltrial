STUDY: An Online Randomized Controlled Trial Comparing the Effects of Mindfulness, Sham Mindfulness and Book Listening Control on Pain Experience in Adults With Recurrent and Chronic Pain
NCT ID: NCT04602286
Sponsor: The University of Queensland

Phase: NA
Design: PARALLEL
Masking: QUADRUPLE
Enrollment: 373

Arm: Mindfulness meditation (EXPERIMENTAL)
Arm: Specific sham mindfulness meditation (SHAM_COMPARATOR)
Arm: General sham mindfulness meditation (SHAM_COMPARATOR)
Arm: Book listening control (NO_INTERVENTION)
Intervention: Meditation (1 x 20-minute guided audio training)

PRIMARY: Pain intensity
  Time: 40 minutes
  Desc: Assessed via a numerical rating scale (0=no pain, 10=most intense pain imaginable)
PRIMARY: Pain Unpleasantness
  Time: 40 minutes
  Desc: assessed via a numerical rating scale (0=no pain, 10=most unpleasant pain imaginable)

ELIGIBILITY:
Inclusion Criteria:

* At least 18 years of age
* Recurrent pain (two or more days in the last month)
* Chronic pain (pain most days in the last three months)
* Able to read and understand English

Exclusion Criteria:

* Not experiencing recurrent or chronic pain
* Incomplete or invalid data (response time \< 32 minutes, failing attention checks)
* Completing the 20-minute training module in \< 18 minutes or \> 90 minutes

SUMMARY:
Both mindfulness meditation and expectancy effects are known to reduce pain intensity, pain unpleasantness and pain catastrophizing, but it is unknown whether and how expectancy effects contribute to the overall effect of mindfulness meditation on these outcomes, especially during significant global events such as the coronavirus pandemic. This study includes four interrelated aims that will probe these effects and interactions.
